description: Turn "I keep having to verdict this same thing" about review-surface units into a checked-in standing approval — probe the specimen, survey every window that shows the same change, find where the decision is already recorded, pick or extend a rule shape until one rule covers that swath (or let two existing rules compose), dry-run the fill, land it, and drive the cycle to READY so the docket never asks again and the surface is sitting-ready without being asked. Use when the user names units or a window and wants them auto-OKed for good, or hands over a docket of approved windows at once — the batch form, which /batch-standing-approvals drives.
argument-hint: "[unit-id …]"
---

Turn a "stop asking me about this" complaint into a rule in `rebuild/standing-approvals.yaml`, applied every cycle by `rebuild/tools/standing_verdicts.py`. The named unit is a specimen of a phenomenon; the job is every window that shows that change, not the specimen's digest family. The tool's module docstring is the authority on what each rule shape proves — every shape in its `SHAPES` table, and the composed reading that lets two rules explain one window between them — so read it first and do not restate its contracts here or anywhere else.

## Hard rules

- The decision is the user's; the rule only records it. Find where it was made (a verdict family, a rune edit, a sitting note) before writing anything, and cite it in the rule's `note:`. Never author a rune `why:` — that voice is the user's — but the `note:` in `standing-approvals.yaml` is agent-written, in the file's own idiom.
- A rule must fill the whole surveyed phenomenon and nothing else. Every shape has a blind spot, stated in its docstring; before landing a rule, list the blank units it would fill and read each one against that blind spot.
- Never hand-write the verdict store or any `verdicts-*.json`; the cycle lands the fill through `merge_verdicts`. Never join unit ids across manifests.
- Counts belong to the dry run's output and the cycle summary, never to prose or the rule's note.
- The sitting ends at READY, unasked. A rule that is written but not landed through a cycle has changed nothing the user can see, so the passes start as soon as the tree is final and run to `make verdict-ready` reading READY — never hand back a rule with the cycle left as an offer.
- If none of the checked-in shapes as they currently match can name the whole survey, extend the matcher in `rebuild/tools/standing_verdicts.py` (and the probe when it enumerates the cells) until one can — that is this skill's job. Teaching extension-dropped a kept `ex-ext-M`, a follower list, or `ex-con-N`, adding slide when two ink-delta digests left the rest of ·See queuing, and adding redrawn when ·Eight's pulled-in bowl fit no pixel contract, were that work. A whole new shape is the last move, still this skill's (tests in the file's idiom, a matcher docstring).

## Steps

1. **Probe the units.** `uv run python rebuild/tools/standing_probe.py u-NNNNNN …` (read-only; reads the live autosave for blankness). Users often name the change rather than a unit, in the corpus `data-expect` minilanguage (`·Tea.half ~x~ ·No` → `·Tea ~b~ ·No`): `doc/data-expect.md` is the grammar, and `parse_expect` in `test/test_shaping.py` is the authority. `--find TEXT` is the route from that prose to a specimen — a plain substring match over every human unit's notation, blanks first and capped with the total stated, narrowed by `--blank-only` and `--limit N` — and with the surface already open in front of the user, `/` in the review app searches the same units while each row's Copy button puts a prompt preamble for that unit on the clipboard, which is the same route with the user's own eyes on it. For each unit the probe prints the two grains side by side — recorded glyphs/cells/seams, and the rendered pieces of both fonts with placement, own-frame origin and cell count, read as "same shape placed N columns over", "redrawn", or "inkless" — then what every checked-in rule makes of it, what the composed reading credits, and how many human units share exactly its ink-delta digests and how they were verdicted. Name every change in the window from that table before going further: a slide (same shape, origin and placement moved), a dropped extension (redrawn by exactly the tail, follower placed closer), a re-spelling (pieces differ, picture does not), or something else. The probe pays one fixed surface load per process unless the standing daemon holds it (`make standing-daemon`; `rebuild/tools/standing_daemon.py`'s docstring is the authority on what it holds and when it declines), in which case a probe or a dry run is a query over its socket that prints byte for byte what the in-process form prints, and `--daemon always` says so when it cannot be served; either way each extra mode or unit id rides one call free and the daemon answers one request at a time, so batch everything the chain has already given you into one call — `--find`, unit ids, `--coverage`, a cell enumeration — with two limits: a bare `--shapes` menu read is the one call that never loads the surface, so nothing else may ride on it, and a flag's arguments must come from a table the probe already printed, never guessed to save a call, because a wrong `ex-ext-N` token enumerates a different survey.
2. **Survey the swath.** The named unit is a specimen. After naming the change, enumerate every human unit that shows it, from the index and without shaping, so it rides the same load as step 1's call: `--survey BEFORE_GLYPH` (or `--survey BEFORE_GLYPH AFTER_CELL`) for any change a before form names — a redraw, a gain, an entry contraction or drop, a stub drop, a join becoming a break — prints every human unit carrying that before glyph, grouped by the before form and the after cell it settles into, then by the left family with the seam change into the pivot, the seam change out of it, and the follower family and cell, each group with its verdict tally and everything in code-point order, and says how many windows carry the glyph but cannot be placed; `--extension-cells` for an exit stretch and `--retarget-cells` for a join that moves or arrives. That set is the job. The specimen's digest family is evidence the user already approved the change; it is not the fill target.
3. **Find the decision's record.** The survey's already-verdicted members usually show the user has already approved the change through echo; `git log -S<token> -- glyph_data/runes/qs<Family>.yaml` finds the rune edit that made it (a narrowed `extend:` list, a dropped record) and its commit subject; a sitting's parked question lives in `WHATNEXT.md` or the batch progress file. If none of these exists, the change is not yet decided — stop and ask, because a rule would be inventing a verdict.
4. **Pick the smallest home, in this order.** A rule's shape is which key its `match.after` carries, never its id; `SHAPES` in `rebuild/tools/standing_verdicts.py` is the map from key to shape. (a) An existing rule that already names the phenomenon and merely lacks a pivot form, follower, cell or digest — extend it, and `uv run python rebuild/tools/standing_probe.py --coverage RULE_ID` re-runs that rule's own survey relaxed of everything it names, so the candidates arrive as a docket with a verdict tally each rather than as a guess. (b) Two existing rules that each explain part of the window — no rule at all: the composed reading fills it once both rules exist, and the probe's `composed:` line says whether it does and which rules it credits. (c) A new rule in the structural shape that names the change: `uv run python rebuild/tools/standing_probe.py --shapes` prints the menu, walked off the `SHAPES` table itself, each row with the symptom its own matcher opens with. Read the probe's rendered-grain reading of the window against that menu, pick the row whose symptom is the change you named in step 1, then read that matcher's own docstring for what it proves and — the part that decides whether the rule is honest — where it is blind. (d) An `ink-delta` rule only when the survey is a single digest — the change never rides beside anything else. Two or more digests for the same change means a companion is minting new identities; a structural shape is required, and if no shape in the menu names the survey as it currently matches, extend the matcher (back to (c)) rather than listing those digests.
   - A shape that names cells has to have them enumerated before a rule in it can be written, and the probe does that enumeration: `--extension-cells PIVOT TOKEN SEAM` for an extension-dropped rule, where `TOKEN` is the `ex-ext-N` the before glyph carried or the `ex-con-N` the after cell carries, and `--retarget-cells PIVOT BEFORE_SEAM FOLLOWER AFTER_SEAM` for a join-retargeted or join-created one. Each prints every pivot cell, follower family and follower cell the rule must name, with a verdict tally per pair, in code-point order — name them all, and set `follower:` to the family list. `--coverage` is the same enumeration turned on a rule that already exists, which is why rung (a) reaches for it. For a shape that names forms rather than cells, `--coverage` runs the survey over the rule's family and reports the before forms, the after forms as the after font names them, and for entry-contracted the left families the rule does not name, each list relaxed while the rule's other lists hold, so a before form settling into a named after form is the docket and the family's unrelated positions are not; `COVERAGE_SHAPES` in the probe is the roster, and the two shapes it leaves out — ligature, which names no forms, and ink-delta, which names digests — make the probe say so.
5. **Write the rule and dry-run it, targeted first.** Append to `rebuild/standing-approvals.yaml`; then iterate with the targeted run, `uv run python rebuild/tools/standing_verdicts.py verdicts-autosave.json --explain RULE_ID --targeted`, which evaluates only the windows the rule could speak for at the name grain — the composed pre-gate's candidate positions for a composable shape, the pivot's presence for the ligature shape, the digest subset for an ink-delta rule — plus any `--unit u-NNNNNN` you list, and prints only that rule's lines: its own line, every composed line that credits it, its rollup line, its tripwire and its `--explain` block, each byte-identical to what the whole-domain run prints for the same rule (`rebuild/test_standing_verdicts.py` holds that over the frozen mini bundle), then one line per listed unit saying whether it is a candidate of the rule and what the surface decided about it, which is how to ask why a specimen is missing from the explain block. It costs about the surface load rather than the domain, writes no fill file and never touches the memo, so an edit to the rule is a re-run and nothing else; its first line says how many units it evaluated, and a rule whose pivot is a common letter evaluates thousands, which is still the cheaper form (`--explain` is single-valued, so a second rule's lines need a second run). Read the rollup, not the own line alone. That is the distinction that misleads — the composed pass claims a window before any single rule is asked about it, so a composable rule's own line counts only the windows no composition took, and a rule reading small there may already be reaching the whole survey through credit. Check the rollup before concluding the matcher needs widening; the run says in its own words when a rule reached nothing at all, and it also flags what a block of numbers hides — a matched unit carrying a verdict outside the approve/either set, which is the shape an over-broad rule takes from outside, and an `except_left` family no window on this surface joins from. The explain block prints the rule's matched unit ids split the same way its line counts them, which is what the coverage check needs: filled plus already-verdicted plus held must cover the survey, held only by `except_left`. Probe the filled units that are not in the survey in one call — the probe takes a list of unit ids and its load is paid once regardless — and confirm nothing else changed in each — an `extension-dropped` rule cannot see a slide or a re-spelling elsewhere in the window, so a fill of that shape is only as honest as the surface's `pair` and `secondary_seams` fields. When the rule reads right, one whole-domain run is the final pass before step 6: `uv run python rebuild/tools/standing_verdicts.py verdicts-autosave.json --out tmp/standing-dry.json --explain RULE_ID --jobs N`, the only form that prints every other rule's line — where a new composable rule shows the windows it moved off a sibling's own line onto a composed line — the tripwire across every rule, and the fill file the cycle would land. `--jobs N` decides the whole domain across a worker pool instead of one unit at a time, byte-identical at any width and whether the daemon serves the run or not; the tool derives no width of its own, so read N off the `plumbing --standing-fill-jobs` line of `make artifact-cycle ARGS='--dry-run'`, which is the width the cycle itself refills at on this box. A rollup short of the survey sends you back to step 4, usually to extend the matcher, not to append another digest.
6. **Test and gate, sized by what moved.** Two branches, and the fork is the diff itself: `git status --porcelain` naming only `rebuild/standing-approvals.yaml` is branch (a), anything more is branch (b).
   - **(a) Rule-only** — the matcher, the probe, and their tests untouched, and no Python in the working tree moved since its last pyright-covered run. A rule landing under an unchanged matcher needs no test of its own, and it does not need the foreground gate either: the only tests whose subject is the checked-in rules file live in `rebuild/test_standing_verdicts.py` (through `load_rules` over the real file), and the plumbing step's own refusal of a checked-in rule that reaches no window is what asserts the same `rule_reach` predicate the step-5 dry run just printed. So run the targeted subset — `uv run pytest rebuild/test_standing_verdicts.py rebuild/test_standing_probe.py --lane contracts -n auto --dist worksteal` — and move on; skip `make prettier` (black is Python-only) and skip the foreground `make test-rebuild`. The full lanes are not waived: both green records key on the rules file's bytes through `rebuild_gate_closure_files` in `rebuild/tools/artifact_cycle.py`, so they re-run — but inside step 7's background gate pass, off the blocking path, and nothing else was ever waiting on this change (gate:make-test exempts `rebuild/` wholesale (`make_test_exempt` in `rebuild/tools/artifact_cycle.py`), and run_m1, the surface build, and gate:conform never keyed on the rules file at all).
   - **(b) Matcher moved** — add the contract cases in the file's idiom: the new contract's positive, its negative, and, if the shape is composable, its composed-walk case; `uv run pytest rebuild/test_standing_verdicts.py -n auto --dist worksteal` is the loop while writing them, not a gate step. Pyright needs no line of its own: the PostToolUse hook in `.claude/settings.json` runs it on every Python write, and `make test-rebuild` runs it again inside the suite. Run `make prettier` in the foreground first, because it rewrites Python that is inside the closure the gate is about to freeze. Then the gate goes detached rather than under a foreground Bash timeout — the constraint was always sequencing, not foregroundness, and a run killed at the tool's ceiling records no green and re-pays that lane in-cycle — with step 7's cycle chained onto it so the green flows straight into the pass that lands the fill, and `&&` so a red gate never reaches the cycle: `nohup caffeinate -i sh -c 'make test-rebuild && make review-cycle SERVE=bg; echo "rc=$?"' > tmp/cycle-pass.log 2>&1 &`, record the pid at launch, judge liveness by `kill -0 <pid>` (never by grepping process names — the xdist controller sits at 0% CPU while its execnet workers grind), and judge the outcome by the recorded `rc=`, not by grepping for any one closing line, of which `rebuild_gate` has several legitimate ones. The cycle half of that chain keeps its own record whatever the redirect catches: `var/build-logs/latest/` holds the resolved plan, a byte copy of the terminal and one log per step, so a step that goes red is read there rather than scrolled for in `tmp/cycle-pass.log`. The window is read-only: drafting the commit message and reading diffs may overlap it, but nothing in the gate's closure may change — the post-run fingerprint re-check declines the green and the whole run is wasted — and the commit itself still waits for the gate to answer, because a red gate must be able to stop it. Land every edit first, gate last.
7. **Start the cycle, then land it, then finish at READY.** The passes are the longest thing left and the only thing that puts the fill in front of the user, so they start the moment the working tree is final — before the commit message is drafted, not after it is approved — and the commit is proposed while they run. Nothing about committing disturbs a pass: every green record digests worktree bytes, and `git commit` changes none of them, so a commit landing mid-pass neither invalidates the run nor is invalidated by it. An _edit_ does disturb it, which is the whole discipline here — the closure is `rebuild/` and `glyph_data/` (`rebuild_gate_closure_files`), so a rule tweak while a pass runs throws the pass away, while `.claude/`, Markdown, `tmp/` and `var/` sit outside it and stay free to move.
   - **(a) Rule-only** — both passes chain in one detached shell, plumbing-plus-census first and the gate re-earn second: `nohup caffeinate -i sh -c 'make review-cycle SERVE=bg ARGS="--skip-gates"; echo "PASS1 rc=$?"; make review-cycle SERVE=0; echo "PASS2 rc=$?"' > tmp/cycle-pass.log 2>&1 &`, recording the pid at launch and judging liveness by `kill -0 <pid>` and outcome by the recorded `rc=` lines. Pass 1 takes the port and cannot be arranged otherwise — the verdict-plumbing key folds in `rebuild/standing-approvals.yaml`'s bytes, so a rule landing always runs the plumbing and writes the store, and `server_may_stay_up` in `rebuild/tools/artifact_cycle.py` grants its reprieve only to a pass that writes neither the surface nor the store — but it is short and the app is back up when it returns (on a tree the last cycle left settled, that is — pending M1 or surface work from unrelated edits folds itself into this pass and lengthens it, which is that work being paid, not the rule's cost). Pass 2 re-earns the rebuild suite's green in-cycle while the letters stay on screen: pass 1's plumbing green rides, so it writes neither surface nor store and leaves the server the port, and a verdict landed in the app between the passes just means it takes the port back and completes rather than refusing. A red pass 2 on this branch is a pre-existing failure being surfaced, not the rule's doing: no other test in the suite reads the rules file. Each pass opens a run directory of its own under `var/build-logs/` and `latest` follows the newer one, so reach for pass 1's by its `<stamp>-<sha>` name once pass 2 has started.
   - **(b) Matcher moved** — step 6's chain already carries the cycle behind the gate, so nothing new starts here; the pass that runs is the plumbing-only one, and it leaves the server up on the port.

   **The commit.** Commit in reader terms ("Stop having to verdict …"), and let it wait on two things only. On branch (b) it waits for the gate's `rc=0`, because a red gate must be able to stop it. On either branch it waits for pass 1's census rewrite when that pass moves one: `rebuild/review-census-pins.json` is the last accepted census and committing its diff is the acceptance, so the rule and the census it moved belong in one commit rather than in a commit and a straggler. If approval arrives before the pass has printed it, commit the rule and put the census diff in its own commit the moment it lands — never leave it sitting in the tree.

   **Finish at READY without being asked.** Between the passes `make verdict-ready` reads NOT READY with honest wording — gates unverified — so the sitting is done only when the log carries pass 2's `rc=0` (branch (b): the chain's) and `make verdict-ready` reads READY. Watch the log to its closing banner — the summary table, then `Cycle complete.` — rather than declaring victory at launch, then report in one go: the rule's fill line from the cycle summary, the census diff in full, and the readiness verdict. If it still reads NOT READY, name the clause that is unmet and clear it — a half-run cycle handed back as an offer is the one outcome this step exists to prevent.

   When any of this does need a foreground Bash timeout sized, size it from bare `make cycle-timings` — recent runs' actual per-step seconds — rather than `ARGS='--by-step'`, whose pooled medians lag what the longest step costs now; no duration written down here would survive the next box, or even this one's drift.

## Batches

A batch here is a docket of approved windows handed over at once — many unit ids, or a whole sitting's approve column — not the letter batch whose progress file step 3 names. Every step above still applies, per phenomenon rather than per window; what changes is the shape of the work. `/batch-standing-approvals u-NNNNNN …` (`.claude/workflows/batch-standing-approvals.js`) is that shape saved. It takes the unit ids as its `args` — an array, a whitespace-separated string, or `{units: […]}` — its invocation is the go-ahead for its lander's per-phenomenon commits, and it runs only on a clean tree: its first agent stops on a non-empty `git status --porcelain` listing, because the lander commits shared files by path and reverts a phenomenon it cannot land by file, and an uncommitted edit already in one of those files would be folded into a commit or discarded. Its stages: one agent probes every unit in one call and clusters the changes by phenomenon, one read-only analyst per cluster validates a rule on a scratch rules file and returns it paste-ready with its decision record and commit subject, a single lander applies them in dependency order, a verifier reads the whole input list back, and the detached gate-and-cycle chain runs last. The workflow is the script; what follows is what it encodes, and what to hold to when driving a batch by hand.

- **Cluster by phenomenon, not by window.** The first pass is one probe over every unit in the batch — the load is paid once, so every id rides one call — then a clustering of the distinct changes its tables print, keyed by position, before glyph, after cell and seam change. A window with two unexplained changes belongs to two clusters. The units are the evidence; the phenomena are the work, and each one gets its own survey — `--survey` for a change a before form names, the cell enumerations for one that names cells — and walks steps 2 through 5 on its own.
- **Balance the clusters.** The analysis phase ends when its slowest member does, so split the phenomena across analysts by the work each one costs — a matcher extension, or a shape whose cells have to be enumerated, weighs more than a pivot form added to a rule that already names the phenomenon — never by how many windows each cluster holds.
- **Analysts validate on a scratch copy and hand over paste-ready YAML.** `--rules tmp/…/rules.yaml` on the probe and on the targeted dry run (`uv run python rebuild/tools/standing_verdicts.py verdicts-autosave.json --rules tmp/…/rules.yaml --explain RULE_ID --targeted`) lets an analyst prove a rule fills its survey — `--survey`, `--extension-cells`, `--retarget-cells` and `--coverage` all take the same `--rules` — without touching the tree, and a rule drafted onto the same scratch copy as an earlier one lets the composed reading be read before either lands. An analyst hands over the rule as it will be pasted, the decision's record from step 3, the matcher extension it needs when it needs one, and the commit subject. The lander's job is then mechanical: paste, one whole-domain dry run (step 5's final pass, read against every rule already landed), the rule-only test subset from step 6(a), commit. A lander that re-probes or re-surveys is doing the analysis twice.
- **Land in dependency order.** A rule another phenomenon composes with lands first, so the second rule's dry run reads the composed line with both halves present; a matcher extension lands with or before the first rule that needs it, under step 6(b), and the rules it unblocks follow as rule-only commits.
- **Concurrency is memory-bound.** With the standing daemon up (`make standing-daemon`), no agent holds a surface: the box holds one, `STANDING_DAEMON_BYTES` in `rebuild/tools/standing_daemon.py`, and the analysts fan out to the cores, each probe and targeted dry run a query that queues behind the one in flight. Without it every probe and dry run loads the surface, a large fixed share of the box that no width derives from, so the cap is procedure: at most three agents hold a surface at once on the smaller box in `doc/fleet.md`, and no agent runs two surface-loading processes at a time. `doc/parallelism.md` says why nothing reads free memory to widen that. The daemon is stopped before the detached gate-and-cycle chain, whose widths do not price it. The targeted test subset takes seconds and is not the constraint; the analysts are the fan-out, and the lander is serial for the tree's sake rather than the box's.
- **A blank the sitting approved can still be undecided.** The batch framing — the user approved every window in the pile — makes step 3 optional for none of them. A window whose survey siblings carry `neither` or `reject` on the same stroke is a conflict to surface, not a rule to write: hold it out of the batch and ask.
- **Gate once, at the end.** Each rule-only commit runs step 6(a)'s targeted test subset and nothing more; the rebuild suite and the cycle run once after the last commit, as step 6(b)'s detached chain — `make test-rebuild && make review-cycle SERVE=bg` — and the sitting ends at READY as step 7 says.

## What this costs when skipped

Each of these was rediscovered by hand once and is why the probe exists: the same change in a different window carries a different whole-window digest as soon as any other visible change rides beside it (a re-spelling that paints no different pixel is inside the digest's grain and does not), so an ink-delta family's approvals prove the decision but cannot fill the composed window — listing the specimen's digest (and one composed companion) leaves the rest of the swath on the docket, which was the first ·See rule; "for real this time" taught slide so every window with that column drop filled, including ones that also carry a blessed extension; the structural extension shape fills blindly past anything that is not a seam, so its dry-run fills must be read, not counted; the rendered fonts position through GPOS, so never reason about a slide from `hmtx` advances — the probe reads shaped placements; a `slide` rule refuses on one stray pixel by design, and a window pairing a blessed slide with a blessed extension drop is the composed reading's case, not a new rule's; and an already-verdicted echo family is evidence for a rule, not a substitute for one — echo only reaches pixel-identical lookalikes, a rule reaches windows the user has never seen.

## Scratch discipline

`tmp/standing-dry.json` and any survey output are scratch and never committed. The durable outcome is the rule, its tests, and the commit message; the fill itself lands through the cycle.
