# Rust port of the M1 settlement kernel — plan

Landed — the port is built and cut over: the Rust kernel is the engine of record (issue #48). [Issue #40](https://github.com/adiabatic/abbots-morton-spaceport/issues/40) was the tracker; its sub-issues carry the milestones as history, and this file stays the design-facts home. It deliberately does not carry measurements: the harnesses beside it regenerate those, and a number written here would be stale the moment the next change lands.

## The decision

Settled and landed: `rebuild/pipeline/table.build_tables` and its settlement engine were reimplemented in Rust as a standalone binary, kernel-only, tracked in issue #40, and the Rust engine is the engine of record.

**The exit bar was relaxed at cutover, and this is the record of that change so nobody re-litigates it.** As first recorded, the bar was two full letter migrations dual-running byte-identical after integration; the cutover happened with one in flight (·Out, its sitting not yet held) and none complete. What stood in for the missing letters: the dual-run window was silent from the day it opened — every `gate:kernel-differential` run and every `gate:kernel-harness` run green, zero reds ever, in a journal that demonstrably records reds for other steps — through the one migration it saw, which was genuine kernel novelty (the first live `bind:`/`contract:` records and the first via-lead ligature, a change both engines learned mid-migration without a byte moving between them); a full `run_m1 --engine rust` then reproduced the Python engine's artifacts byte for byte end to end, engine stamp aside. The deciding observation: the window's one real defect — dead decision-table transitions both engines enumerated identically — was caught by `gate:conform`, which the cutover keeps on every cycle, while the differential necessarily waved it through; the check that binds correctness is independent of which engine is of record, so a second letter's marginal evidence was judged not worth its wait. The two-letter count itself had never been argued anywhere in the record, only asserted.

**What the cutover landed.** The default engine flipped to `rust` and its artifacts became the blessable set (a `--engine python` run now refuses the green record, symmetrically). `gate:kernel-differential` inverted to on-demand: keyed on kernel code alone — a rune edit feeds the twin engines identical new input and cannot make them disagree — and building both of its sides fresh, since the artifacts on disk are now the kernel's own fold. `gate:kernel-harness` was deleted outright, its per-letter deep pass with it, leaving the standalone harnesses (`make kernel-fixpoint` and friends, `make kernel-liveness`, `make kernel-differential`) as the instruments for kernel-semantics edits. `trace_memo.py` was deleted rather than ported, as planned below. The write-it-twice tax on `settle.py` survived the cutover (see the design facts), policed by conform every cycle and the differential on demand. Issue #78 then deleted the Python fixpoint outright, and with it every harness whose job was comparing the two; retiring the Python settlement engine paid off the rest of the tax and took the last of those harnesses with it, and the spec-ingest parity has since folded into the contracts lane (`rebuild/test_kernel_io.py`), so `make kernel-gate` is `kernel-check` alone today. The Disposal section records what went.

**It is not a seconds-per-day decision.** The port's incremental value over the keep-the-Python stack is real but modest. What settles it is alphabet headroom: the fixpoint grows steeply in the alphabet — `scaling/scaling.py` is the live authority for how steeply — and the target is all 44 codepoint-bearing letters.

**Read the exponent in a stated denominator, always.** `scaling.py` prints its consecutive-pair exponents against _runes_ and then the whole-ladder least-squares fit in both denominators, so the figure worth quoting is on the page rather than something to fit by hand — but which of the two bases it came from still has to be said out loud. A rune exponent is the letter exponent multiplied by `d ln letters / d ln runes`, which the nested ladder drives from below 1 to above 1 as it stops adding ligatures and starts adding letters, so quoting a rune-denominated figure as a per-letter one overstates the 44-letter multiplier severalfold; and the ladder's letters-to-ligatures mixture is the _inverse_ of the migration's, so the two bases bracket rather than agree. Quote the whole-ladder fit rather than any one consecutive pair, and read the apparent rise across consecutive pairs as a property of which letters that rung added rather than of how many.

**The user's rationale, recorded:** all 44 letters eventually. That is what makes the port a _when_ rather than an _if_ — and also what makes it insufficient on its own, because at 44 letters even a parallel port lands near the edge of tolerance, and only at the optimistic end of both the multiplier and the exponent bands.

## Sequence

1. **The Python levers first**, and not mainly for their own sake. They move the denominator every port measurement is a ratio against; benchmarking a port against the pre-lever kernel overstates its value by the whole stacked lever factor, so each lever was measured against a comparison tree built at an older revision rather than against a remembered figure. They are also what keeps the next few migration batches bearable. **Landed**: the live `run_m1` batch freezes its inherited heap and disables cyclic GC, and the settlement memoizations are in the shipped kernel. Two were measured and deliberately _not_ taken — a twelve-element memo key that cost more than the call it replaced, and one inside the noise — and git's copy of `levers/apply_m1_patches.py` is where they are written down; that harness and the comparison trees it patched are in git history with the rest of the Python-fixpoint scaffolding (see Disposal).
2. **Re-baseline.** Done on the M1 Pro MacBook Pro (see `doc/fleet.md`): the journal exists, the endpoints, the sweep and `kernel-model/run.sh` were all re-run after the levers landed, and `evidence/raw/perf2/verify-headline/classify_commits.py` re-derives the arming rate from the git history rather than assuming it. Re-run the endpoints and the sweep after each migration batch; `make cycle-timings` is the live authority for the cycle itself.

**The kernel model's job is done.** The fidelity drift this step flagged — the real kernel memoizes `candidates` and `transition_trace`, the model does not — is retired along with the projection it fed: the decision no longer rests on a modeled multiplier, and the port is measured against the real kernel through the differential contract (the boundary sub-issue under the tracker), not projected from the model.
3. **Decided.** Issue #40 and its sub-issues are the plan of record; the thresholds below stay as the record of what the argument turned on.

## Thresholds that settled it

The decision is made; this table is its record, and one row keeps a live job. The whole-ladder exponent stays the migration's early warning — re-run `scaling/scaling.py` after each batch, because a port moves the constant and not the exponent, so a steepening ladder makes work avoidance due regardless of language. The kernel-semantics cadence row is spent: the tax it priced was retired outright rather than waited out.

| Threshold                                                               | How to check                                                                                                                                                                                                         | What it means                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Alphabet target passes ~18 letters                                      | the migration itself                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Every keep-the-Python path is exhausted; only a port reaches further                                                                                                 |
| Six-config cold wall passes ~600 s                                      | `levers/kernel_all_configs.py`, or `make cycle-timings`'s `build_tables_total` — which since the crate's fold landed covers a fold the `enumerate-configs` arm does not, so the two compare on the enumeration alone | The levers are spent                                                                                                                                                 |
| Whole-ladder exponent rises above ~4.5 **in letters**, or ~5.5 in runes | `scaling/scaling.py` after each migration batch, fitted over every rung and printed in both denominators                                                                                                             | Even a parallel port stops reaching 44; work avoidance becomes mandatory regardless of language                                                                      |
| Peak RSS at the top rung passes ~half the box                           | `scaling/scaling.py`'s `rss_high_water_gb`, now each rung's own kernel process high-water, and `kernel_all_configs.py`'s `peak_rss_gb` — both decimal GB, measured through `rebuild/tools/peak_rss.py`               | Memory, not wall clock, becomes the binding constraint — and a port moves that constant without moving its exponent                                                  |
| Kernel-semantics commits fall below ~1/quarter                          | `git log -- rebuild/kernel-rs/src/`                                                                                                                                                                                  | The duplication tax collapses and the port gets much cheaper — overtaken by retiring the Python settlement engine, which collapses the tax to nothing at any cadence |
| `TraceShare` can be made value-keyed                                    | see the open question below                                                                                                                                                                                          | Parallelism and the share stop excluding each other; every parallel figure rises                                                                                     |

The exponent check is the cheapest and the most decisive, and it is the one nobody was running. Re-run it after each migration batch. Two cautions, both learned by getting them wrong: fit the **whole ladder** rather than quoting a consecutive-pair figure, because a single pair is sensitive enough to ordinary run-to-run scatter to swing by a large fraction of the threshold, and which letters that rung happened to add moves it further than how many did; and state the denominator, per the decision section above. The letters and runes thresholds in that row are the same threshold, not two.

The alphabet row is stated against the historical six-config budget and moves with it — at a 20-minute tolerance the keep-the-Python paths reach several letters further, which is why "what wall are we willing to wait" is worth deciding explicitly rather than inheriting.

## Design facts

These came out of measuring the port rather than guessing at it, and they are the reason the project is smaller than it looks.

- **The kernel boundary is frozen, and `rebuild/pipeline/kernel_io.py` is its home — the module and function docstrings there are the contract.** The dump the kernel reads is `ams-m1-spec/1`, canonical JSON of the whole `ResolvedSpec` with collection order preserved because it is load-bearing; the stream it returns is `ams-m1-transitions/1`, the full `Transition` grain (with `joint` still the trace's own floor, since the prospect-divergence pass runs in the crate's fold) plus the fired provenance, the deep-class map, and the reachable cells. What a build crosses that boundary for is now the tables rather than the stream — `kernel_exec.build_table_files` → the three artifact families → `table.read_windows` and `table.read_treaty_tsv` — and the stream survives without a consumer — `kernel_exec.enumerate_transitions` → `FixpointProduct` still carries the grain a table drops, but no build stage and no tool asks for it, and the rebuild suite is what keeps the path exercised. `table.table_digest` is the one scalar that says two builds agree at full contract grain, and it is a second implementation of the crate's now rather than the only one, kept because `rebuild/test_windows.py` checks the crate's recorded digest against it.
- **The cut line is `build_tables`, not the existing subprocess.** `run_m1` looks like a boundary but also mints glyphs, runs defect gates, emits GSUB/GPOS, compiles the font with fontTools and runs three HarfBuzz gates. One level in, a JSON dump of the resolved spec carries the input side. The port first cut the return side at the enriched transition stream, with Python keeping the rule fold, the treaty fold and the artifact writers — and the reason given was that because the writers never changed hands, byte-identity of the persisted artifacts was free. **That half is superseded: the writers did change hands, the crate folds the product where the worklist leaves it, and byte-identity of every artifact is the contract because it was proven across the change rather than because nothing moved.** What is left on this side per configuration is the gzip the windows artifact wears, a head read and a treaty TSV parsed. No FFI either way.
- **PyO3 is dominated, not merely worse.** Its only advantage over a file boundary is saving a serialization measured at a small fraction of the build, and passing Python objects across the boundary rebuilds the very object graph the packing exists to delete.
- **`spec_load.py` is outside the runtime closure** and does not need porting.
- **`trace_memo.py` was deleted at cutover rather than ported.** At port speed the persisted memo's whole value collapses to a few seconds, which is less than the gzip and JSON it costs.
- **The rule fold moved too, and its ordering is the thing to guard.** `_rules_for_input` was the largest and hairiest function in `table.py`, and the argument for leaving it in Python was that it costs a fraction of a percent of runtime. What overruled that was the stream: keeping the rule fold on this side meant keeping the 450 MB of transitions it reads. It lives in `rebuild/kernel-rs/src/rulefold.rs` now, and because its output _is_ the shipped GSUB rule ordering — invisible to every count-based check — `settlement-<config>.tsv` and `M1.generated.fea` are where a divergence in it shows.
- **The risk concentrates in the liveness probe** (`liveness.rs`, whose specification is the Python `_ProspectLiveness` git history holds). Most of the runtime lives in one module that is simultaneously the hottest, the subtlest and the easiest to get silently wrong: an under-opened liveness verdict omits windows, and the font is then wrong in a way only the conformance sweep catches — which is why that sweep keys its settle memo on the raw window and shares nothing with the crate's verdicts.
- **The verification story was unusually strong and is the best argument for having attempted it at all.** The Rust-vs-Python differential harnesses were a small piece of work because the Python fixpoint already diffed itself against its serialized enumeration; they did their job across the dual-run window and retired with that fixpoint at issue #78. What remains, and is what bound correctness all along: gate:conform shapes the compiled font through HarfBuzz and compares it against a re-settle of every swept string, window by window. That re-settle reaches the crate now, so what the gate proves is the transcription and the sufficiency of the window abstraction rather than a second engine's agreement — and its memo keys on the raw window, which keeps it independent of the crate's enumeration and fold.
- **The port retires `settle.py`'s engine.** Keeping a Python twin for independent verification meant writing every settlement change twice, and that recurring duplication was the port's largest real cost — larger than the build cost within a few quarters. It was paid down in three moves and then paid off: issue #78 gave the enumeration — the worklist, the liveness probes, the fibers — one home in the crate; issue #79 moved shipped formation onto the crate's guard sweep; and retiring the settlement engine gave the semantics one home too. `conform.py` re-settles every swept text through the crate's `settle-cases` verb per raw window, `settle.py` keeps the vocabulary — tokens, boundary cells, guarded formation — and a settlement change is written once. What still has two sides is the spec contract the crate ingests, held byte-aligned by the contracts lane's spec-echo parity test.
- **The author-facing settlement path reads the engine of record.** `explain.py`, `probe.py`, and the review surface batch their sequences through the crate's `settle-cases` verb, whose full trace includes the paste-ready resolve stubs and refuse rationale that the panel quotes. There is no Python settlement behind them to disagree: the crate answers the panel, the sweep and the tables alike. Reproducing any build or explanation therefore requires both `uv` and `cargo`.
- **Rust, not Go.** Rust's multiplier holds flat across problem size while Go's decays as its collector scans a larger live heap. A memoized fixpoint whose entire working set stays live until the build ends is the worst shape for a tracing collector and the best for an arena.
- **The packing is the win, not the language.** CPython beats both Rust and Go at inserting a ten-slot _string_ key, because it caches string hashes. The same insert on a packed integer key is an order of magnitude faster. A port that keeps string keys forfeits most of the prize. Relatedly, a fast non-cryptographic hasher without a proper finalizer was measured _far slower_ than SipHash on these keys, whose low bits are a five-value alphabet.

## What a port does not fix

At the full alphabet the conformance sweep is Θ(alphabet^horizon), and no language change touches that exponent. `--conform-horizon` and the calt sweep's `max_chars_after` are coverage decisions that move numbers a rewrite cannot reach. Persisted liveness verdicts are the other lever of this kind. If the port happens, these are still needed; if the exponent keeps rising, they matter more than the port.

## Open questions

- **Does cross-configuration sharing still pay at port speed?** Answered at cutover: no. No share was ported — the kernel enumerates configurations independently in parallel — and the Python share was deleted with the trace memo, which also retires the `TraceShare` value-keyed threshold row above.
- **What did the class-grain deep slots actually buy?** Measured as a one-flag A/B (`AMS_DEEP_CLASSES=0` against the default), the collapse is real in rows and growing with the alphabet, but it does not reach the clock at today's size: per-window cost rises by almost exactly the collapse factor. The estimate that motivated it was an order of magnitude larger than what the ladder shows, so the case for it should be restated in terms of what fewer rows are worth downstream — a smaller memo to persist, fewer rows for the conform sweep and the review surface — which is real and is not measured anywhere yet.
- **Does the ladder's exponent describe the migration's?** The nested subsets freeze the ligature count partway up, so letters outrun runes; the migration does the reverse. Nothing measures a large alphabet's ligature load, and the two bases differ by several-fold at 44 letters. Counting reachable cells for the target alphabet from the registry would replace the widest guess with an estimate, and costs no machine time: cells predict windows, and windows predict CPU almost linearly.

## Disposal

Per the repo's note-taking rules, checked-in evidence lives only as long as its decision is open, and the port's decision is closed. What the tree keeps: `scaling/`, the migration's early-warning system, which runs every rung through `ams-m1-kernel` so the peak it reports is each rung's own process high-water and the seconds are the kernel's (a row recorded under the Python fixpoint compares on exponent and not on constant); `levers/`, which prices the thresholds above through `kernel_all_configs.py` and holds the font-build, spec-load, signature, surface and YAML levers; and the `cut-the-work`, `ink-and-tsv` and `primitives` benches. What stands in the pipeline: `rebuild/pipeline/table.py` is the data model, the artifact readers and the digests, and folds nothing; `rebuild/pipeline/kernel_exec.py` is the seam; `make kernel-gate` is the crate's own fmt/clippy/test gate (`kernel-check`) and nothing else, because there is no second settler or second enumeration to differ against; the spec-ingest parity check is `rebuild/test_kernel_io.py`'s spec-echo test in the contracts lane, and the ladder is `rebuild/tools/scaling_ladder.py`; and the conformance sweep fails fast on a stale enumeration, as the witness gate does.

Everything else lives in git history rather than in the tree: `evidence/`; the Python fixpoint — `table.py`'s enumeration half (`_ProspectLiveness`, the slot filters, `_WindowOptions`, `_DeepFiberDeriver`, `_enumerate`) and its fold half (`assemble_tables`, the prospect-divergence pass, the rule and treaty folds, the windows writer, the table-level asserts), with `rebuild/tools/prospect_divergence.py` and the inventory nothing read; every harness whose job was comparing that fixpoint with the crate — `rebuild/tools/kernel_fixpoint.py` with its `make kernel-fixpoint` family, `kernel_gate.py`, `kernel_liveness.py`, `kernel_differential.py`, the `test_kernel_seam` / `test_kernel_engine` / `test_kernel_gate` / `test_kernel_liveness` files that drove them, `run_m1 --engine python`, and the conformance sweep's in-process fallback; the Python six-configuration endpoint `levers/m1_all_configs.py` with its slicers and profilers (`m1_slice.py`, `probe_subset.py`, `prof_slice.py`, `prof_variant.py`) and the comparison-tree machinery they measured through (`mktree_at.sh`, `mkvariant.sh`, `apply_m1_patches.py`); the port-scoping instruments that sat beside the sweep (`scaling/{closure,funcsizes,liveness,loc,loc2,portscope,specdump,split}.py` and `split-k12.txt`); `ram/`, whose two attributions (`attr_fixpoint.py`, `attr_fold.py`) asked where the deleted halves' peaks lived; and the three Python-kernel experiments whose questions the port closed — `compilers/` (mypyc, Cython, PyPy), `freethreaded/` (3.14t) and `kernel-model/` (the three-language model).

Executed when the Python settlement engine retired: `rebuild/pipeline/settle.py`'s `Engine` and everything under it (ranking, refusals, prospect, the trace memo, the late-formation guard's Python half) and `rebuild/pipeline/specificity.py` outright, leaving `settle.py` the vocabulary — tokens, boundary cells, `tokens_from_codepoints`, and `form_ligatures` reading the crate's guard verdicts. The differential went with the twin, having nothing left to differ: `rebuild/tools/kernel_differential.py`, `export_settlement_corpus.py` and `fuzz_settlement_corpus.py`, their `make kernel-differential` target, and `rebuild/test_settlement_corpus.py`. `rebuild/test_specificity.py` retired into `rebuild/kernel-rs/src/specificity.rs`'s `mod tests`, where §15.5's paranoia budget is spent now, with the real-YAML shapes pinned in `rebuild/test_spec_load.py`; the settlement claims of `rebuild/test_settle.py` re-point at the crate through `kernel_exec`, and the white-box ones landed as crate `#[test]`s. `make kernel-gate` is `kernel-check`.

What stays has a job the crate does not make moot: `levers/kernel_all_configs.py` is the endpoint's arm of record, `scaling/` is the early warning, and `primitives/`, `ink-and-tsv/` and `cut-the-work/` price ports and coverage levers that have not happened.
