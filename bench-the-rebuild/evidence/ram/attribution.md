# RAM attribution runs (issue #51)

The three attribution runs the #50 sub-issues are gated on, measured 2026-08-16 on the M1 Pro MacBook Pro (10 logical cores, 32 GB, CPython 3.14.7, Darwin 25) at the tree state of commit `25c09880`. Instruments, named once for every figure below: 1-frame tracemalloc for allocation attribution and requested-bytes peaks (compression-blind), and `getrusage` peak RSS through `rebuild/tools/peak_rss.py` for high-water marks — bytes in the raw files, decimal GB in prose. `raw/` holds each run's full row (top-40 sites each; at k=24 the top 40 cover 2.83 of the 2.84 GB retained, so the grain loses nothing that matters), and the harnesses under `../../ram/` re-run any of them — `AMS_ATTR_TRACE=0` for the untraced arm. A traced run's RSS includes the tracer's own bookkeeping, so every RSS figure quoted below is from an untraced arm unless said otherwise; traced peaks are comparable across runs because they count what was asked for. This is the proof pile for the still-open sub-issues it cites; per the repo's note-taking rules it goes when they close.

## Run (a) — Peak B, the shipping fold path (`raw/attr-fold-default*.json`)

First measurement of `run_m1._fold_stream`'s halves, one configuration (default, 1,400,761 windows): `kernel_io.read_transitions` peaks at 0.60 GB, and `assemble_tables` carries the process high-water to 1.23 GB. So Peak B is ≈1.2 GB per configuration — the fold is not where the build's RAM lives, and sub-issue #55's "≈1 GB off Peak B" estimate should be re-sized against this before landing anything. The split the traced arm gives: the reader retains 0.54 GB in exactly two shapes — 2.74 M `Settled` objects at `kernel_io.py:271` (263 MB, two per window; #53's interning target) and 1.40 M `Transition` rows at `kernel_io.py:277` (235 MB; #59's `slots=True` target) — while assembly retains almost nothing on top (its end state is 0.57 GB) yet doubles the traced peak to 1.13 GB in flight. Assembly's cost is transient grouping copies, which is precisely the side of the line #55's `by_input`/`expanded_pairs` levers work.

## Run (b) — the fixpoint at k=20 and k=24 (`raw/attr-fixpoint-k2{0,4}*.json`)

k=24 is the whole current alphabet (24 runes), and its default-config fixpoint measures 8.53 GB high-water at 1,400,761 windows — 6.09 kB/window. k=20 gives 3.19 GB at 577,014 windows (5.54 kB/window; the ladder's rung compositions shifted when ·Out landed, so this k=20 is not the old snapshot's k=20). Traced, k=24 peaks at 7.46 GB requested with 2.84 GB still retained after `build_tables` returns — the retention a finished configuration leaves for #56 to evict, and its shape confirms the tracker's levers at production weight, by site:

The other 4.6 GB between traced peak and retention is transient working set; attributing ownership _at_ the peak (rather than what survives it) is what memray — now a dev dependency — is for, if #53/#54 want the transient half split before landing.

## Run (c) — the suite fixtures per xdist worker (`raw/attr-fixtures*.json`)

The bare `workload` load (246,939 units) measures 1.41 GB high-water — the fixture docstring's ≈0.8 GB is confirmed stale, as #58 records. The full per-worker materialization — load plus enrichment, exactly the shape a session-fixture worker holds — measures 6.83 GB. Attribution: the load is the audit row model (`audit.py:92–93`, 4.2 M tuple/`AuditRow` allocations each — #59's `AuditRow` target), and enrichment adds one large `EnrichedUnit` payload per unit (`enrich.py:906`, 354 MB) plus re-materialized settle diagnostics (`ranked` dicts at `settle.py:1281`, traces at `:1341`, `Elimination`s at `:691`, explain reports at `explain.py:96`) — the same explain-only shapes #54 targets in the build, alive again in the suite. Corroboration from the now-routine instrumentation rather than a harness: the rebuild gate run during this work printed per-worker peaks of 3.3–11.9 GB across ten workers on its new peak-RSS summary line, which is #58's containment case measured on every run from here on.

## The tracker's open tension, reconciled

Tracker #50 flagged that 8.4 kB/window × 1,400,761 windows extrapolates the per-config fixpoint to 11–12 GB while the six-config process measures 8.94 GB. The direct measurement dissolves it: one full configuration's fixpoint high-water is 8.53 GB, i.e. 6.09 kB/window. The 8.4 kB/window slope was fitted over ladder rows whose RSS column was a process-lifetime cumulative max — each rung charged with all its predecessors, the measurement bug this sub-issue fixed in `scaling.py` and `m1_all_configs.py` — so the extrapolation was inflated; the six-config figure was never in deficit. Darwin page compression is not needed to close the gap, and the residual between requested and resident is ordinary allocator overhead in the ordinary direction (7.46 GB traced peak vs 8.53 GB resident, ≈14%). One question this run deliberately leaves to #56: how much of the 2.84 GB a finished configuration retains is per-configuration versus shared across configurations through the trace share — a single-config harness cannot split that, and the eviction lever's value depends on it (the six-config process peaking at 8.94 GB, barely above one configuration's 8.53 GB, says most of it must be shared or freed, not stacked six-fold).
