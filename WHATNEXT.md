# What next

## The main thread — M1 rune migration

The rebuild migrates the cursive-join engine rune by rune and checks each batch against the old font through the oracle.

**Next step: the qsYe batch is closed; the qsWay batch builds green and awaits its review session.** `rebuild/M1-BATCH28-PROGRESS.md` (qsWay and its ligature qsWay_qsUtter) holds the parked questions, the recorded design overrides, and the verification recipe. `make verdict-ready` is the readiness check, `glyph_data/runes/` lists the migrated runes, and `make novelty-order` orders the remaining blanks.

**Where the state lives:** `make verdict-ready`, and the same status as a banner in the app, reports whether the corpus, gates, and verdict store are ready: corpus freshness by input fingerprint and against the M1 font the review app is serving, gate status, verdict-store alignment, the fullest verdicts file, blanks remaining, and the server. `rebuild/out/cycle_summary.json` is the last cycle’s record: corpus units and duplicate groups, unmatched oracle rows, each gate’s status, and the `verdicts-carried-<sha>.json` it wrote with the number of verdicts it carried. The per-gate summaries under `rebuild/out/m1/` hold the rest (`conform_summary.json`, `manual_pins_summary.json`, `readback_summary.json`, `oracle_summary.json`). The store is `verdicts-autosave.json`, and `verdicts-journal.ndjson` logs every write to it. A verdict recorded after the last carry is in the store but in no carried file until the next carry. `make complaint-list` clusters the open reject and neither verdicts by the rune records that decided them. Read those files; don’t copy their numbers into this file, where they go stale at the next cycle.

**What the gates check**, described here instead of as numbers: the crate’s string replay checks every build’s tables against its own engine over every string up to the conformance sweep’s maximum length (`run_m1.REPLAY_MAX_LENGTH`; `rebuild/out/m1/replay_summary.json` is its record). The conformance sweep is exact: zero divergences at maximum length 4 over the settlement configurations, and over every text of up to two letters in the ss10 overlay, which read-back’s isolation check extends to every text. The per-edit sweep also runs the split-buffer check, and `make conform-deep` runs it at maximum length 5 and above. Read-back checks that the font holds the planned rules and that its boundary glyphs are inert, and the build’s witness stage checks that every rule is reachable by settling the certificate the crate writes beside it. The Manual pins replay clean under a gate that allows no waivers, scope included. The M1 GSUB uses type-7 Extension offsets (`m1_settle` is `useExtension`), and shared-ClassDef packing (`rebuild/pipeline/pack_gsub.py`) keeps the settlement lookup within the subtable-offset headroom floor that `readback.SUBTABLE_OFFSET_HEADROOM_FLOOR` enforces. The oracle’s remaining unmatched rows are divergences from the old font that verdicts decide, not failures. Among them is the ss03 ·Fee·Tea divergence: the rune extends ·Fee’s x-height exit by 1, per the old derive’s stated intent, and the divergence is accepted through verdicts with no ledger entry. The complaint list’s older complaints are windows already argued and accepted, so a _new_ complaint is the signal. After its carry and merge, the artifact cycle applies the duplicate prefill and the checked-in standing approvals (`rebuild/standing-approvals.yaml`), so only new questions reach the queue. Duplicate groups are keyed on the localized ink change, so unchanged flanking letters, including followers that only moved because an advance width changed, are not part of the key.

## Open forks in the gates

**How should a gate see a rule that matches across a ZWNJ?** HarfBuzz skips a ZWNJ that a context class does not name, and the ZWNJ lock leaves a rune without an entry (·She and some ligatures) raw after a ZWNJ, so for those windows the emitted rule order is the only protection. The shipped-order walk matches `uni200C` literally and cannot see the skip, so such a fault surfaces only in the conformance sweep or the deep sweep, and not at all when it needs a text longer than the deep sweep's maximum length. The candidates are an emit-time scan that first-matches every real window containing a ZWNJ under the skip, with each raw-after-ZWNJ glyph placed after it, and moving the crate's third-slot boundary row ahead of its block's fourth-slot rows (`rulefold.rs`), as the other slots already do.

**Should a class marked `ink_identical: true` hold rows whose placed ink differs?** The oracle enforces the flag only through its position check, which compares glyph origins and advances (`_position_mismatch` in `rebuild/pipeline/oracle_positions.py`), so a redraw that keeps every origin and advance passes it. The name-grain classes `bare-name-live-join` and `dangling-anchor-dropped` hold rows whose placed ink, as `rebuild.review.ink.InkComparator` compares the old Senior OTF with `M1.otf`, differs from the old font's: among them the ss03 full ·Tea after ·They+Utter or after ·Cheer and a letter with an x-height exit, the contraction before ·J’ai that the rebuild puts on the other glyph of the junction, and the ·X·Way·Cheer·Gay and ·X·Way·Cheer·No rows of the next question. `zwnj-word-initial-unification` holds the ·Way·Cheer·It·Roe rows, whose redrawn ·Roe the `qsRoe.en-ext-1-at-5` alias hides from the name grain and the `roe-baseline-bar-kept-after-it` standing approval covers. Each of those rows with a `.noentry` name sits beside its plain sibling, the same window without the name, which shows the same difference, and the author accepts them as they stand. The candidates are a placed-ink check over the rows such a class takes, refusing a row whose ink differs either in every name-grain class or only in the `.noentry` fall-through of `oracle.classify_divergence`, and an explicit exclusion for each hidden redraw, like the one `zoo-entry-contraction-renamed` has for ·It·Roe.

**Should the position check blame a shift on a kern earlier in the row?** `_position_mismatch` counts a mismatch as the kern's when every mismatching slot follows a kern-attributable slot, one whose old advance carries a kern or that sits next to a ZWNJ, and a row in a single ink-identical class keeps that class when its mismatch is the kern's and no class takes the row with the mismatch. In ·X·Way·Cheer·Gay and ·X·Way·Cheer·No, where the old font kerns the lead ·X against ·Way, the one-pixel shift from ·Gay's entry contraction or ·Cheer's exit contraction therefore counts as that kern's, and the rows stay in `dangling-anchor-dropped`, while ·Way·Cheer·Gay, ·Way·Cheer·No, ·Cheer·Gay, and ·Cheer·No, with the same shift and no kern before it, are unmatched. The question is whether a mismatch with a cause of its own at a later junction should count as the kern's.

**Does the lane split cost the 12-core machine time?** A gated cycle splits the cores between the build lane and the gate lane (`memory_budget.split_cores`), so on the 12-core machine the corpus build runs narrower than the widest pool it has run there, while gate:make-test's pool and, under the queue policy, gate:rebuild-contracts' pool run wider. Only the 18-core machine has timed the split. Time a `--fresh` pass under each `--rebuild-pool` policy on the 12-core machine and read the step that ends each pass as the growth in the ended-last tally of `make cycle-timings ARGS='--critical-path'`, whose rows also count passes run at other gate:make-test widths, and the corpus build's units phase in `ARGS='--inner'`; if the build lane ends those passes and its corpus-build row runs longer than on that machine's earlier full passes, the build lane's share is the width to revisit.

## The review server through a pass

**The land awaits its first real passes.** A pass keeps the review server running: it builds beside the served corpus and lands the corpus and the store together (`doc/review-cycle.md` § The server and the pass). No automated test drives a browser across a land, so the first real passes are the end-to-end check: two open tabs verdicting during the build, the verdict update and the land, a tab closed and reopened mid-pass, a Ctrl-C during the land, a `kill -9` of the land's child inside its locked section, and a `merge_verdicts --restore-as-of` replay compared against the store and the orphan documents. The store's lock hold time is the land step's `[t] land-locked` line (`make cycle-timings ARGS='--inner'`), and `LAND_BYTES` is re-measured from the land step's peak. Issue #204 (a worktree pass beside the main checkout's server) is answered by the same design: a worktree pass's verdict update writes only its scratch store, its land takes the worktree store's own lock, and `/capabilities` names the main checkout's server as another checkout's, so the pass leaves it alone and a worktree's first pass, which builds in place, is not refused by it; it closes once a worktree pass confirms that.

## Open questions from the requirements

`doc/core-idea.md` records the rebuild’s requirements, and `doc/rebuild-design.md` settles the questions its interviews raised and keeps to its decisions, except the ones below.

**Does each rune’s own ductus gate its migration, in place of finishing all of the ductus first?** Core-idea makes finishing the whole ductus a precondition for the rewrite (its section “The stance set’s truth is held jointly by ductus _and_ bitmaps”), and `doc/rebuild-design.md` §3.1 makes each rune’s migration wait on that rune’s own ductus instead. §15 item 10 records the departure and what the per-rune order gives up. The departure waits on the author’s sign-off.

**Can a stylistic set lift a refusal?** In core-idea a stylistic set adds capability: an unlock enables a join that no stance declares by default, as ss05 does for ·Tea’s both-baseline pairing, and taste vetoes are a separate layer on top of what is allowed. Whether a set can also lift a veto is undecided. The rune grammar cannot express such a lift: a `when:` condition’s `feature:` names a single active `ssNN` (`featureTag` in `rebuild/schema/rune.schema.json`), so a `refuse` can be limited to configurations where a set is on, but never to those where it is off.

**How do extensions that are not for joins behave?** Core-idea makes a join extension a parametric adjustment that a stance owns, and the rebuild’s `extend` and `contract` records (`doc/rebuild-design.md` §3.3) always target an entry or exit at a named height. Whether an extension made for some other reason works the same way is undecided.
