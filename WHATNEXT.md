# What next

The living punch list for the Abbots Morton Spaceport rebuild — the forward-looking companion to the per-milestone plans under `rebuild/`. It records what ought to happen next, the open forks in the road, and the deferred work we have consciously parked. Keep it current: see “Keeping this file honest” at the bottom.

## The main thread — M1 rune migration

The rebuild is migrating the cursive-join engine rune by rune, validating each batch against the old shipped font via the oracle.

**Current frontier: the qsSee batch is built, and its sitting has not been held.** The rune, the ligature rune (qsSee_qsUtter, the batch's formation-closure obligation), the alphabet, the aliases and the gates all landed; `rebuild/M1-BATCH9-PROGRESS.md` carries the batch's recorded design overrides and the families of windows the sitting has to decide, and `make verdict-ready` is the standing check. The qsEt sitting before it closed by declaration — its fresh rejects had already been consumed into rune changes during the sitting, and with no "the old way is better" verdicts left to make, the remaining blanks were bulk-approved with a `[bulk-close]` note on every record, on the qsRoe/qsI precedent. Adjudicating the qsSee docket is what closes this batch; `glyph_data/runes/` is the roster of what has already migrated.

**Migrating a letter takes four hand-updates that nothing prompts for**, three of which fail by producing a confidently wrong number rather than an error, so do them before trusting any oracle figure: `rebuild/m1-aliases.yaml` (an entry per old compiled glyph name — for the new rune _and_ for already-migrated neighbors whose old forms only ever appear beside it), `rebuild.review.census --update`, and three hardcoded artifact counts in the rebuild suite that grow with the alphabet (`test_review_enrich`'s subset row count, `test_review_build`'s echo-group sibling count, and `test_emit`'s two `@m1_form_guard_qsDay_qsUtter` class memberships). The subset refilter that used to lead this list is off it: `run_m1` now runs `baseline_subset.ensure_fresh` before anything else, so an `M1_ALPHABET` edit can no longer feed the oracle stale subset tables.

**Where the state lives, so this file need not carry it.** `make verdict-ready` — and the same status as a banner in the app — answers whether the surface, gates, and verdict store are ready: surface freshness by input fingerprint, gate greenness, verdict-store alignment, the frontier carry, blanks remaining, server. `rebuild/out/cycle_summary.json` is the last cycle’s own record (surface units and echo groups, unmatched oracle rows, each gate’s status, and the `verdicts-carried-<sha>.json` it wrote with how many verdicts rode it); the per-gate summaries under `rebuild/out/m1/` hold the rest (`conform_summary.json`, `boundary_equivalence_summary.json`, `manual_pins_summary.json`, `budget.json`, `oracle_summary.json`); the store itself is `verdicts-autosave.json`, journaled in `verdicts-journal.ndjson`, which the newest verdicts ride until the next cycle’s carry; and `make complaint-docket` clusters the open reject/neither verdicts by the rune records that decided them. Read those; don’t copy their numbers back into this file, where they go stale the next time a cycle runs.

**The standing shape of the gates**, which is what a number here would have been trying to say: the conform sweep and the boundary-equivalence sweep are exact — zero divergences, full rule and transition coverage; the Manual pins replay clean under the no-waiver gate; and the budget gate’s yellow flag is its steady state rather than a warning, because the M1 GSUB rides type-7 Extension offsets (`m1_settle` is `useExtension`). The oracle’s remaining unmatched rows are verdict-gated divergences from the old font, not failures — among them the deliberate ss03 ·Fee·Tea divergence, where the rune extends by 1 per the old derive’s stated intent and the divergence lands through verdicts with no ledger entry. The docket’s standing complaints are windows already argued and accepted, so a _fresh_ complaint is the signal. The artifact cycle lands the echo prefill and the checked-in standing approvals (`rebuild/standing-approvals.yaml`) itself after its carry+merge, so only genuinely new questions ever queue — and echo groups key on the localized ink delta, which puts unchanged flanking letters, including followers that merely slid over by an advance change, outside the grain.

**The live decisions a later batch must not re-litigate.** Each rune’s own `why:` is the authority; this is the index. ·Pea·No renders `·Pea.!half ~b~ ·No.alt` in every window: half-·Pea’s x-height exit no longer reaches ·No (a `qsNo` carve-out on the `toward:` scope), paired with the matching ·Pea carve-out in qsNo’s x-height-exit prefer, which otherwise wins the seam back before ·Day/·No/·It/·Oy/·Fee and — with only the qsPea half — turns ·Pea·No·Oy into a break rather than a low join. The accepted price is ·Pea·No·Fee and ·Pea·No·Oy, which stop after ·No instead of chaining on at the x-height. ·Fee cannot join both to and from at the x-height, and that is structural rather than policied: qsFee is the clockwise `loop` (top-entry stub, x-height exit) plus the counter-clockwise join-born `reversed-loop` (`require: [entry]`, no exits). qsFee prefers its forward x-height join over the backward one when entered from ·May/·No/·Low — except before ·Tea and ·Utter, where the approved taste keeps the backward join — paired with the matching qsFee carve-out in qsMay’s follower prefer; the engine raises E-INCOMPARABLE if either side widens without the other. qsFee’s exit row admits the qsX_qsUtter ligatures (extend-by-1 before ·Day·Utter via the lead), so ·X·Fee·Day·Utter joins. The ·Pea·May-family windows deliberately keep the un-extended baseline join and ride the may-baseline-entry-extension-dropped class instead of matching the old font’s en-ext-1.

·X·Day·Tea·Day, ·X·Day·Tea·It and ·X·No·Tea·It regroup like the old font. The ·X·Day·Tea·No windows render their third form (`·X·Day | ·Tea.!half ~b~ ·No.alt`), and ·Day·Tea·No·dot rides along because `boundary` includes the namer dot. Word-initial ·No·Utter·May joins on both sides via ·Utter.alt as a strict gain — qsUtter’s before-·May follower prefer paired with the matching carve-out in qsNo’s boundary prefer, again E-INCOMPARABLE if either widens without the other — while ·No·Utter·Day stays with the old font, restoring the plain “alt-·Utter only before ·Low or on strict gain” principle. qsDay’s yielding prefer is one unscoped record; qsNo carries the scoped baseline twin, before ·Tea·It; ·Day also yields before ·Tea·No at a run end; and full ·Tea joins ·No.alt at the baseline through qsTea’s `order: [full, half]`. Read the follower sets off the runes — they grow with every migration.

**The depth-4 fork stands as landed.** In the default config, X₀·Day·Tea·Utter·Tea·X₁ and ·Oy·Tea·Utter·Tea·X₁ render the predecessor yielded and ·Tea~·Utter joined for every baseline-enterable X₁; ·Pea/·Tea/boundary followers keep the rescue, a following ·Oy does not (·Tea·Oy ligates, so the chain’s third hop stops matching), and ss03 keeps its y5 escape and joins ·Utter on both sides. The chain grammar is depth-4 — nested `then:` chains, capped at three hops past `right` — with the cap and the table’s fourth slot derived from `model.RIGHT_WINDOW_SLOTS` and an import-time arity assert; the runes that carry such a chain are whichever prefer records nest a `then:`, and that set grows batch by batch, so read the runes rather than a list here. ·Oy takes the shape without qsDay’s `self: {entry: live}` scope, so word-initial ·Oy·Tea·Utter·Tea moves off the old font as well — the entry-liveness split was built and passed over, and the divergence stands adjudicated “either” on both arms. ·Day·Tea·No·Utter keeps the ·Day·Tea join per the recorded follower-of-·No scope; its approve-with-note is still worth setting in the app.

## Deferred and tracked follow-ups

Nothing gates these — act on any cycle, whenever it’s worth it.

Route the copy through an injectable hook or skip it under pytest.

### Do when a gate takes too long

Not gated on a named event — these come due when a cycle step gets slow enough that acting pays off. "Slow enough" is measured, not remembered: every cycle appends host-tagged per-step wall times to `rebuild/out/cycle-timings.ndjson`, and `make cycle-timings` (`ARGS='--by-step'` for per-machine medians) is where to look before pulling any lever here. The lever inventory lives in [the speed-up tracking issue](https://github.com/adiabatic/abbots-morton-spaceport/issues/7), whose sub-issues cover each cycle step.

- **Conform-gate cost** — `gate:conform`, the exhaustive font-vs-settle sweep, runs at horizon 5 on every cycle whose conform inputs moved (`--conform-horizon` on the cycle driver, passed through to `run_m1 --conform-only`; `--skip-conform` opts out; on unchanged inputs a green record auto-skips it, like every heavy cycle stage — `--fresh` forces a full rerun). It no longer sits in the look-edit-look loop’s critical path: a `make review-cycle` pass that rebuilds anything defers it to the next pass, so the sweep’s cost lands on the converging pass rather than between an edit and seeing the letters — which is what makes the horizon lever below a question about how long a sitting waits to start, not about iteration speed. The decision tables are no longer part of what the gate spends: run_m1 serializes each configuration’s window enumeration beside its other artifacts (`table.write_windows` → `windows-<config>.tsv.gz`, stamped with `fingerprint.tables_value` over the sources the fixpoint read) and each sweep worker opens its own, so the fixpoint is paid once by the build; the gate rebuilds it unconditionally only when the stamp fails to match, which means the runes moved after the last build. Settlement and the first-matching-rule replay are memoized per distinct normalized window (`conform._SettledWindowWalk`): the kernel runs once per window and every recurrence across the sweep’s texts is a dict probe, so a stream’s marginal cost is the uharfbuzz shape plus the raw-label replay and the settle term scales with the table’s size rather than the sweep’s redundancy — the memo’s soundness rides the same slot-relevance rules the build’s partition gates assert, alarmed by `rebuild/test_conform.py`’s walk-equivalence sweeps. The sweep term grows with the fifth power of the alphabet and shrinks with `--conform-horizon` — witness top-ups keep rule and transition coverage exact at any horizon; only off-corpus diff density shrinks. The past-horizon top-ups are dominated by committed-left prefixes deeper than the sweep, and they still grow with the enumeration even though building it has left the gate — but both deep slots are pruned at the source: `table.third_slot_filter` and `table.fourth_slot_filter` enumerate right3 and right4 only where some own-rune chain is still unknown over the nearer slots, since a chain that already resolved cannot be flipped by a deeper token. That keeps the deep window blocks scaling with the authored chains rather than the alphabet — a new depth-3-carrying rune adds only its chain-live windows, not an alphabet-wide fan-out per (right1, right2) pair. When the gate becomes the cycle’s long pole again, the lever is `--conform-horizon`, decided deliberately.

## Keeping this file honest

This is a punch list of what’s _left_ — not a changelog of what happened. When work lands, edit the current-state description in place and delete the status it supersedes; never append a dated `Update (…)` paragraph. What got done — with what counts, and under which commits — is already in the git log, so this file records only the live frontier: the open forks, the next batch, and the consciously-parked follow-ups. When a landed change leaves a genuinely-open residue, record the residue as a forward bullet, not the change that produced it. The broader workflow rules — no after-the-fact reports, bounded progress files, evidence that outlives its decision — live in `AGENTS.md`’s “Note-taking and the rebuild logs” section; `AGENTS.md` points back here.
