# What next

## The main thread — M1 rune migration

The rebuild migrates the cursive-join engine rune by rune and checks each batch against the old font through the oracle.

**Next step: the qsYe batch builds green and awaits its review session.** `rebuild/M1-BATCH27-PROGRESS.md` holds the parked questions, the recorded design overrides, and the verification recipe. `make verdict-ready` is the readiness check, `glyph_data/runes/` lists the migrated runes, and `make novelty-order` orders the remaining blanks.

**Where the state lives:** `make verdict-ready`, and the same status as a banner in the app, reports whether the corpus, gates, and verdict store are ready: corpus freshness by input fingerprint and against the M1 font the review app is serving, gate status, verdict-store alignment, the fullest verdicts file, blanks remaining, and the server. `rebuild/out/cycle_summary.json` is the last cycle’s record: corpus units and duplicate groups, unmatched oracle rows, each gate’s status, and the `verdicts-carried-<sha>.json` it wrote with the number of verdicts it carried. The per-gate summaries under `rebuild/out/m1/` hold the rest (`conform_summary.json`, `manual_pins_summary.json`, `readback_summary.json`, `oracle_summary.json`). The store is `verdicts-autosave.json`, journaled in `verdicts-journal.ndjson`, which holds the newest verdicts until the next cycle’s carry. `make complaint-list` clusters the open reject and neither verdicts by the rune records that decided them. Read those files; don’t copy their numbers into this file, where they go stale at the next cycle.

**What the gates check**, described here instead of as numbers: the crate’s string replay checks every build’s tables against its own engine over every string up to the conformance sweep’s maximum length (`run_m1.REPLAY_MAX_LENGTH`; `rebuild/out/m1/replay_summary.json` is its record). The conformance sweep is exact: zero divergences at maximum length 4 over the settlement configurations, and over every text of up to two letters in the ss10 overlay, which read-back’s isolation check extends to every text. The per-edit sweep also runs the split-buffer check, and `make conform-deep` runs it at maximum length 5 and above. Read-back checks that the font holds the planned rules and that its boundary glyphs are inert, and the build’s witness stage checks that every rule is reachable by settling the certificate the crate writes beside it. The Manual pins replay clean under a gate that allows no waivers, scope included. The M1 GSUB uses type-7 Extension offsets (`m1_settle` is `useExtension`), and left-edge shared-ClassDef packing (`rebuild/pipeline/pack_gsub.py`) keeps the settlement lookup within the subtable-offset headroom floor that `readback.SUBTABLE_OFFSET_HEADROOM_FLOOR` enforces. The oracle’s remaining unmatched rows are divergences from the old font that verdicts decide, not failures. Among them is the ss03 ·Fee·Tea divergence: the rune extends ·Fee’s x-height exit by 1, per the old derive’s stated intent, and the divergence is accepted through verdicts with no ledger entry. The complaint list’s older complaints are windows already argued and accepted, so a _new_ complaint is the signal. After its carry and merge, the artifact cycle applies the duplicate prefill and the checked-in standing approvals (`rebuild/standing-approvals.yaml`), so only new questions reach the queue. Duplicate groups are keyed on the localized ink change, so unchanged flanking letters, including followers that only moved because an advance width changed, are not part of the key.

## Open questions from the requirements

`doc/core-idea.md` records the rebuild’s requirements, and `doc/rebuild-design.md` settles the questions its interviews raised, except the ones below.

**Can a stylistic set lift a refusal?** In core-idea a stylistic set adds capability: an unlock enables a join that no stance declares by default, as ss05 does for ·Tea’s both-baseline pairing after ·Et, and taste vetoes are a separate layer on top of what is allowed. Whether a set can also lift a veto is undecided. The rune grammar cannot express such a lift: a `when:` condition’s `feature:` names a single active `ssNN` (`featureTag` in `rebuild/schema/rune.schema.json`), so a `refuse` can be limited to configurations where a set is on, but never to those where it is off.

**How do extensions that are not for joins behave?** Core-idea makes a join extension a parametric adjustment that a stance owns, and the rebuild’s `extend` and `contract` records (`doc/rebuild-design.md` §3.3) always target an entry or exit at a named height. Whether an extension made for some other reason works the same way is undecided.
