# What next

## The main thread — M1 rune migration

The rebuild migrates the cursive-join engine rune by rune and checks each batch against the old font through the oracle.

**Next step: the qsOwe batch, the last letter, builds green and awaits its review session.** `rebuild/M1-BATCH38-PROGRESS.md` holds the parked questions, the recorded design overrides, and the verification recipe. Every letter of the alphabet is now migrated, and the build settles seven configurations (`conform.SETTLEMENT_CONFIGS`, `ss07` among them). `make verdict-ready` is the readiness check, `glyph_data/runes/` lists the migrated runes, and `make novelty-order` orders the remaining blanks.

**Each batch closes with a close-out.** A close-out folds the store's accepted verdicts into the tracked `rebuild/accepted-units.txt` and starts the journal again (`doc/review-cycle.md` § The accepted list and the close-out), and the `close-out` skill runs it, ahead of the next Add when the two come together. The last close-out, which closed the ·Exam batch, keeps its archive under `var/keep/close-outs/`: the store it found, with the notes and provenance markers of the verdicts it accepted, until the next close-out replaces it. With no batch closing, one is due before the journal's first event turns `journal.HISTORY_DAYS` days old, and retention's report and `make verdict-ready` warn once it turns `journal.CLOSE_OUT_DUE_DAYS` days old.

**`REPLAY_PEAK_BYTES`, `deep_replay.DEEP_REPLAY_PEAK_BYTES` and the scaling series wait for the run-time optimization work, which would make readings taken before it stale.** The two constants have their tools on the M5 Pro MacBook Pro (`make memory-series ARGS='--holder replay …'` and `var/keep/ram-inventory/v4/change21-readings/probe.py`), and `rebuild/scaling-series.txt` keeps its ·-ing readings.

**`ORACLE_ABSORB_BYTES` waits for absorb readings from passes that load their settle memos.** Its value in `rebuild/tools/artifact_cycle.py` comes from absorbs on passes whose ranges refused their settle memos because the memos listed the ·Vie+Utter ligature the registry dropped (#624), and its comment has the readings. A memo that lists an outcome the spec cannot name loads with the rows holding it dead, and a full replay rewrites it whole. Once the oracle-absorb row of `make job-costs` holds absorbs from passes that load their memos, the constant takes its comment's rule over those readings, and its comment and the reason `doc/parallelism.md` gives for it sitting above `ORACLE_SHARD_BYTES` change with it.

**Kerning compiles, the review app edits it, and the old font's values carry over.** A rune's `kern:` block reaches the font's `kern` lookup, the gates check it, and a kern edit rebuilds only the font (`run_m1 --kern-only`). The review app's kerning view (`#view=kern`, `rebuild/review/README.md` § The kerning view) draws every facing pair in a witness from the build's `kern-inventory.json`, writes the author's changes into the rune files' `kern:` blocks after checking them as the build would, journals them for undo in `var/kern/`, and holds them while a cycle pass runs. The importer carries the old font's Senior kerning over as a starting point: `uv run python -m rebuild.tools.import_kerning` is a dry run that writes `var/kern/import-report.json`, and `--apply` writes the records and `var/kern/imported.json`, after which the view marks each pair it set as carried over until the author reviews it. What is left is the author's: refining the values in the view, and ruling on what the report lists, the joined groups the old rules moved (dropped, or a `contract`), the facing pairs whose members the old rules gave different values, and the ligature pairs that take their last letter's records through inheritance where the old font left them at 0. `rebuild/KERN-PLAN.md` holds the design the author ruled on (every step of its order of work is built; the text box that settles typed text waits) and two open questions: where a glyph with no rune file keeps its kerning, and how the records reach Junior. A pair across two or more ZWNJs kerns in HarfBuzz and not in CoreText, since the lookup's ZWNJ rule names one.

**Where the state lives:** `make verdict-ready` and the app’s banner report whether the corpus, the gates, and the verdict store are ready, `rebuild/accepted-units.txt` holds the verdicts the last close-out accepted, `rebuild/out/cycle_summary.json` is the last cycle’s record, and `doc/note-taking.md` § Counts and drifting state and `doc/review-cycle.md` name the other records.

## Open forks in the gates

**How should a gate see a rule that matches across a ZWNJ?** HarfBuzz skips a ZWNJ that a context class does not name, and the ZWNJ lock leaves a rune without an entry (·She and some ligatures) raw after a ZWNJ, so for those windows the emitted rule order is the only protection. The shipped-order walk matches `uni200C` literally and cannot see the skip, so such a fault surfaces only in the conformance sweep or the deep sweep, and not at all when it needs a text longer than the deep sweep's maximum length. The candidates are an emit-time scan that first-matches every real window containing a ZWNJ under the skip, with each raw-after-ZWNJ glyph placed after it, and moving the crate's third-slot boundary row ahead of its block's fourth-slot rows (`rulefold.rs`), as the other slots already do.

**Should the oracle also refuse a row of an `ink_identical: true` class whose placed ink differs?** The review build already lists each such unit under the change group of what it shows (`change_groups.reroutes`), so every “Looks identical” class in the app holds only units whose picture matches the old font, and the review-facts pins count the re-routed units per ledger class (the volatile block's `unmatched_groups.rerouted`), which shows a broken identical claim as a number. The oracle still files those rows under the identical classes in `rebuild/out/m1/divergence-audit.tsv`, because it enforces the flag only through its position check, which compares glyph origins and advances (`_position_mismatch` in `rebuild/pipeline/oracle_positions.py`), and a redraw that keeps every origin and advance passes it. The candidates are leaving the oracle as it is, with the re-routed counts as the signal, and a placed-ink check over the rows such a class takes that refuses a row whose ink differs, which would leave those rows unmatched in the audit too.

**Should the position check blame a shift on a kern earlier in the row?** `_position_mismatch` counts a mismatch as the kern's when every mismatching slot follows a kern-attributable slot, one whose old advance carries a kern or that sits next to a ZWNJ, and a row in a single ink-identical class keeps that class when its mismatch is the kern's and no class takes the row with the mismatch. In ·X·Way·Cheer·Gay and ·X·Way·Cheer·No, where the old font kerns the lead ·X against ·Way, the one-pixel shift from ·Gay's entry contraction or ·Cheer's exit contraction therefore counts as that kern's, and the rows stay in `identical-unused-join-name`, while ·Way·Cheer·Gay, ·Way·Cheer·No, ·Cheer·Gay, and ·Cheer·No, with the same shift and no kern before it, are unmatched. The question is whether a mismatch with a cause of its own at a later junction should count as the kern's.

**The build lane's share is the width to revisit on the 12-core machine.** A gated cycle splits the cores between the build lane and the gate lane (`memory_budget.split_cores`), and the corpus build's units pool keeps the build lane's share whenever the gate lane still has work as the pool starts. On the 12-core machine, a pass that runs gate:conform, as a `--fresh` pass does, still has gate:conform and gate:rebuild-contracts running when the units pool starts, so it does not reach the widening for an idle gate lane. A pass that runs neither gate:make-test nor gate:conform holds gate:rebuild-contracts until the units pool finishes, so it reaches the widening there too; the probe pair that checks this on the 18-core machine (issue #306) has yet to run on the 12-core one. The build lane ends a `--fresh` pass there under either `--rebuild-pool` policy, and the units phase at the build lane's share runs far longer than at every core less its parent. The gate lane does not keep its own share busy through that phase. Under the overlap policy, gate:rebuild-contracts runs one worker wide after gate:make-test ends, and under the queue policy the gate lane empties before the units pool does. Issue #506 has the timings; `make cycle-timings ARGS='--critical-path'` and `ARGS='--inner'` read them from the journal.

## The review server through a pass

**The land's end-to-end checks are open.** A pass keeps the review server running: it builds beside the served corpus and lands the corpus and the store together (`doc/review-cycle.md` § The server and the pass). No automated test drives a browser across a land, so these checks are made by hand on real passes: two open tabs verdicting during the build, the verdict update and the land, a tab closed and reopened mid-pass, a Ctrl-C during the land, a `kill -9` of the land's child inside its locked section, and a `merge_verdicts --restore-as-of` replay compared against the store and the orphan documents. The store's lock hold time is the land step's `[t] land-locked` line (`make cycle-timings ARGS='--inner'`). Issue #204 (a worktree pass beside the main checkout's server) is answered by the same design: a worktree pass's verdict update writes only its scratch store, its land takes the worktree store's own lock, and `/capabilities` names the main checkout's server as another checkout's, so the pass leaves it alone and a worktree's first pass, which builds in place, is not refused by it. A pass in a second checkout bears that out: the first pass in a scratch clone, run beside the main checkout's server, names that server as another checkout's, leaves it running and sends its tabs nothing, and builds its corpus in place. A clone exercises nothing specific to git worktrees, so the issue closes once a worktree pass confirms the same.

**The carry's marker strip awaits the first pass that carries the store onto a new stamp.** The carry copies each note as written, less any `[carried …]` marker in its leading marker run (`verdict_notes.drop_carried_markers`). A pass that leaves the store on its stamp merges into it, and the merge keeps the store's record unless the incoming one has a strictly newer `at` (`merge_verdicts.merge_into`), so the store's notes keep their markers until a pass carries the store onto a new stamp. After that pass, the landed `verdicts-autosave.json` should hold as many records as before it and no `[carried` marker outside the units a tab still on the old corpus saved during the pass, since such a tab sends the note its input shows, marker included; the `[standing:` and `[duplicate-fill` markers the fills write from then on should survive two more stamp moves. A close-out keeps no note on the verdicts it accepts, so these checks read the records recorded since the last close-out. The land holds no note, so the markers do not bear on `LAND_BYTES` (`rebuild/tools/artifact_cycle.py`), which the synthetic land series sets; the next letter batch's end, at least as large as ·Gay's interval, reads the land row of `make job-costs` against it. A pass just after a close-out lands a store holding only what the close-out left, so its land row measures nothing a batch's end reaches. Once no tab opened before that pass remains, remove the outbox's allowance for the markers (`CARRIED_PREFIX` and `heldByServer` in `rebuild/review/static/outbox.js`, and the case in `rebuild/review/jstests/outbox.test.js` that covers it) and the “carry's provenance prefix” clause of rule 2 in `rebuild/review/verdict_store.py`; removing them early costs only a replay, which rule 2 applies.

## The review app's kinds of change

The items below that move unit ids or counts do so on purpose, so each of them is a pass of its own, with its expected deltas written down before it runs.

**Retire the classes that reach no unit and fold the ones the change groups cover.** `joins-next-letter-after-zwnj` and `join-height-after-zwnj` reach no unit and stay only as records of the rulings `split-by-space-or-zwnj` relies on; they can retire into its `why`. `spacing-at-kerned-pairs` and `new-joins-near-tea-oy` can fold into the change groups, whose clusters hold the same pixel changes. Each needs the oracle to change with it.

**Rename the oracle's class strings and narrow its predicates.** The oracle and `rebuild/test_conform.py` still name the classes by their engine ids, which the ledger's `match.predicate` values map to the reviewer-facing ids. The rename goes with narrowing the predicates that reach further than their rulings: `half-day-closer-at-baseline-join` takes half-·Day after any letter, `ss03-tea-oy-ligature-kept` keeps a dormant namer-dot ·Day·Utter branch, and `identical-unjoined-from-left` takes any no-entry rename. Until then, a new row one of them takes is listed under a ruled class, which the Queue never shows.

**Move `may-closer-at-baseline-join`'s highlight onto the join into ·May.** In most of its units the amber band marks the unchanged `·May ~x~ ·J’ai` pair. `highlight` is in the unit id, so the fix re-keys those units, in the store and in the accepted list, through a forward map (`rebuild/tools/rekey_verdicts.py`). The map has to be made for this move, joining on everything but the highlight, since the re-key's own fallback join requires an equal one.

**Fix the per-unit summaries.** Some units fall back to the false “only the boundary marker's glyph changed”, some say “settles as a different cell”, and some quote rune record indexes such as `policy.refuse[4]`, which go stale when a record is inserted. `summary` is outside the unit id, so these fixes re-key nothing.

**The author rewords the `why:` in `glyph_data/runes/qsTea_qsOy.yaml`.** It names a group by an id no corpus has. Agents never write a rune `why:`.

## Open questions from the requirements

`doc/core-idea.md` records the rebuild’s requirements, and `doc/rebuild-design.md` settles the questions its interviews raised and keeps to its decisions, except the ones below.

**Does each rune’s own ductus gate its migration, in place of finishing all of the ductus first?** Core-idea makes finishing the whole ductus a precondition for the rewrite (its section “The stance set’s truth is held jointly by ductus _and_ bitmaps”), and `doc/rebuild-design.md` §3.1 makes each rune’s migration wait on that rune’s own ductus instead. §15 item 10 records the departure and what the per-rune order gives up. The departure waits on the author’s sign-off.

**Can a stylistic set lift a refusal?** In core-idea a stylistic set adds capability: an unlock enables a join that no stance declares by default, as ss05 does for ·Tea’s both-baseline pairing, and taste vetoes are a separate layer on top of what is allowed. Whether a set can also lift a veto is undecided. The rune grammar cannot express such a lift: a `when:` condition’s `feature:` names a single active `ssNN` (`featureTag` in `rebuild/schema/rune.schema.json`), so a `refuse` can be limited to configurations where a set is on, but never to those where it is off.

**How do extensions that are not for joins behave?** Core-idea makes a join extension a parametric adjustment that a stance owns, and the rebuild’s `extend` and `contract` records (`doc/rebuild-design.md` §3.3) always target an entry or exit at a named height. Whether an extension made for some other reason works the same way is undecided.
